# Next experiment: test one delivery channel manually

Recommend a one-week, at-most-$200 manual channel test with one participating bar, conditional on operator permission and a usable channel. Do not build the app yet. Another 20 interviews would not establish whether you can actually bring customers.

This is a training scenario. The five conditional statements are synthetic, not real demand evidence. Even if real, “we would pay if you bring 50 customers” would leave both delivery and buying terms unresolved.

## T1/T9. Decision, evidence, and risks

Decision: whether there is a credible acquisition-and-delivery path worth another small test, not whether to fund a large app.

Evidence card S1:

- Paraphrase: they would pay conditional on 50 extra Tuesday customers.
- Type / signal: hypothetical intention / conditional buying outcome.
- Question context: not supplied.
- Interpretation: an attractive result has been named; no channel, price, approval, or achieved result is demonstrated.
- Unknowns: owner authority, real prior spending, why Tuesdays matter, unit economics, and whether this was a leading pitch.

The team has no audience or tested delivery channel. No current-promotion spending or approvals are recorded. Missing baseline is not evidence of zero spend.

| Risk | Uncertainty and stakeholder |
|---|---|
| Acquisition/delivery — immediate priority | Can a reachable local channel motivate people to actually attend? Local patrons, channel owner, and bar all matter. |
| Demand/priority | Is Tuesday demand worth paying to improve versus other problems? Bar owner/operator. |

Provisional segment: one independently operated bar with Tuesday spare capacity in one walkable neighborhood, plus people who already use a local events channel to choose nearby outings. This narrows the experiment; it is not evidence about the broader market.

## Recommended experiment

**Hypothesis:** A single local events channel can generate trackable Tuesday attendance at a cost worth investigating further. We are not testing or promising delivery of 50 extra customers.

**Owner and timing:** Founder owns bar terms and the decision; one teammate owns placement and measurement. Assign actual names before launch. Day 1–2 setup, Day 3–5 channel exposure and Tuesday attendance where the calendar permits, Day 6–7 reconciliation and review. Anchor the seven-day window so it includes a Tuesday after setup. If that is impossible, report a setup-only outcome; do not claim an attendance test occurred.

**Method, proposed only:**

1. With one willing bar, reconstruct its last quiet Tuesday and most recent promotion: attendance, promotion cost, results, staffing, buying authority, and how outcomes were measured. Ask for comparable Tuesday totals the bar is permitted to share. Find out the maximum economically acceptable acquisition cost from its actual contribution economics; do not invent it.
2. Agree in writing on date, ordinary opening hours, truthful event description, capacity, staff contact, and a lightweight check-in tally. The bar must approve the listing and data collected. Do not promise attendance or require a new discount/event expense.
3. Seek one relevant neighborhood events newsletter or community channel with a disclosed audience and a paid placement costing at most $150. Confirm permission, timing, price, and ability to use a unique booking/check-in code before committing funds. Availability is unknown; do not spend just to use the budget.
4. Use an existing free booking form and a distinct code for that channel. Staff tally code-bearing arrivals and total attendance, with no unnecessary personal data. Ask arrivals whether they already planned to visit; this is self-report and only a rough incrementality indicator.
5. Reconcile actual placement cost, exposure if the channel supplies it, bookings, cancellations, code check-ins, total attendance, staff effort, and any recorded purchases. Keep reservations separate from attendance. Compare against prior comparable Tuesdays while noting weather, events, and ordinary variation. A one-night comparison does not establish causal lift.

**Budget ceiling:** placement ≤$150; printed check-in materials ≤$10; optional local travel ≤$20; contingency ≤$20. Total cash ≤$200, including fees. Use free existing tools. No coupons, hidden bar subsidies, or paid software outside this cap. Track founder/staff time separately; the budget assumes unpaid team time and a bar willing to participate without a participation fee. If those assumptions fail, reduce scope or use the fallback.

**Terms and commitment:** A free research test could involve bar staff time and permission to measure, which is participation evidence, not payment evidence. Any later paid pilot needs its own concrete scope, fee, result definition, authority, limits, and acceptance. No 50-customer guarantee is offered.

T8 ledger before launch:

| Requested step | Current status | Meaningful cost | Completion evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bar approves scope/date and assigns staff contact | Not asked | Staff time and permission | Written agreement, then actual staffed measurement |
| Channel accepts dated placement within cap | Not asked | Team cash; channel inventory | Accepted terms, then published placement and receipt |
| Paid follow-on pilot | Not asked; outside this test | Agreed money/time if later suitable | Terms, acceptance, delivery, and payment recorded separately |

Once scope and timing are real, ask explicitly: “Would you agree to this measurement plan and assign a staff contact by [date]?” Record acceptance or refusal separately from completed staff participation. Actual owner names, dates, and conditions remain blank until agreed. This plan is not an authorization to launch, spend, contact anyone, or promise results.

## Predeclared observations and decisions

Before launch, write down the bar-specific acceptable acquisition-cost range and measurement rules. It is an operating criterion derived from this bar's economics, not a universal validation threshold.

| Observed result | Interpretation and next decision |
|---|---|
| No permissible, timely channel or participating bar within the cap | Operationally blocked; spend no placement money. Learn the specific access/terms barrier rather than calling demand disproven. |
| Placement receives little or unknown exposure | Channel setup is inconclusive. Diagnose reach before judging the offer or bar demand. |
| Meaningful documented exposure but no bookings | This audience/message/offer failed to motivate a measurable response. Inspect fit and revise once narrowly; do not build the app. |
| Bookings but no code check-ins | Attendance delivery failed in this run. Investigate friction or tracking failure; reservations are not customers. |
| Check-ins occur, but most already planned to attend or measurement is unreliable | Incremental demand remains unresolved. Improve attribution or run a repeat/control design before charging for extra customers. |
| Plausibly incremental attendance at a tolerable measured cost | Justifies a repeatable channel test and a specific paid offer discussion. Does not prove 50-customer delivery, repeatability, or a scalable market. |
| Attendance arrives at clearly unacceptable cost | Revise the channel or economics; pause this delivery approach if no credible adjustment exists. |

Cost per code check-in is placement spend divided by check-ins; it is not proven incremental-customer acquisition cost. If there are zero check-ins, report that fact rather than a misleading finite cost.

## Fallback, next questions, and review

If the primary channel is blocked, use the remainder of the week for a zero-spend channel-access check: identify a viable local channel, obtain actual terms and exposure information when separately authorized, and compare costs with the bar's economics. Reconstruct one prior promotion with the operator and staff. This can resolve access and baseline gaps but cannot answer attendance delivery; label that question untested. Do not replace it with an arbitrary interview quota.

Next three learning questions:

1. What happened in the bar's last Tuesday promotion—spend, attendance, contribution, and who approved it?
2. Which reachable local audience actually chooses Tuesday outings through this channel, and where does its journey from listing to arrival fail?
3. What measurable result and terms could an authorized payer accept for a subsequent limited pilot?

At Day 7, the founder and product/business teammate review source records together using T10: evidence for each question, counterevidence, unsupported claims, stakeholder differences, belief changes, and the next action with owner/date. Stop spending when the cap is reached or permission/measurement fails. Move from a learning question once useful evidence answers it; continue targeted learning on the remaining uncertainty.

The delay cost of more general interviews and an app build is a week or more spent without testing the main constraint. This small test exposes that constraint while preserving the ability to revise.
